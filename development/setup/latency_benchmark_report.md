# Latency Benchmark Report

**Date:** 21 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Compare report-generation latency for template-based and LLM-based approaches under cold-cache and warm-cache conditions.

## 1. Executive summary

Both approaches meet the target of **p95 latency under 30 seconds**.

- **Template-based reports** are very fast: median latency is 0.18 seconds with cold cache and 0.02 seconds with warm cache.
- **LLM-based reports** take longer: median latency is 3.25 seconds with cold cache and 3.16 seconds with warm cache.
- The LLM report-generation stage is the main latency bottleneck.
- All runs succeeded: 10 reports OK and 0 not OK in each condition.
- These results measure latency and completion, not factual accuracy or overall report quality.
- Confirmed LLM cost for the 20 reports was $0.01 total, averaging $0.0005 per report.

## 4. Cold cache vs warm cache

### Template approach

- Median falls from **0.18 s** to **0.02 s**.
- p95 falls from **0.69 s** to **0.24 s**.

### LLM approach

- Median falls from **3.25 s** to **3.16 s**.
- p95 changes from **5.91 s** to **5.98 s**.
- Research median falls from **0.19 s** to **0.01 s**, but report-generation median rises from **3.04 s** to **3.14 s**.

The warm-cache LLM p95 is slightly higher than the cold-cache p95. With only ten runs per condition, this small difference may reflect normal run-to-run variation rather than a meaningful regression.

## 7. What each latency metric means

### Total latency metrics

- **Total seconds:** The elapsed time for a report run, from the start to completion, measured in seconds.
- **Median:** The middle runtime after sorting all runs from fastest to slowest. About half the runs are at or below the median and half are at or above it.
- **p95 (95th percentile):** A measure of slower-run performance. Roughly 95% of observed runs finish at or below this latency, while the slowest 5% take longer. With only 10 runs, this estimate is coarse and can be sensitive to individual runs.
- **Worst:** The maximum observed runtime in that benchmark group; it is the slowest run seen, not a guarantee of the maximum possible runtime.

### Per-stage latency metrics

- **Plan:** Time spent planning which steps the report-generation workflow should perform.
- **Research:** Time spent retrieving or preparing the company information used by the report.
- **Verify:** Time spent checking or validating gathered information.
- **Report:** Time spent producing the final report or narrative. In the LLM benchmark, this is the dominant stage.

The logs show stage medians and worst values separately. Since each stage's median is calculated independently, stage medians do not necessarily add up to the total median.

### Cache conditions

- **Cold cache:** Runs made with the benchmark's cold-cache setup. Relevant cached results may need to be fetched or computed again.
- **Warm cache:** Runs made with the benchmark's warm-cache setup. Relevant results may already be cached, reducing repeated research work.

The logs do not specify exactly which caches were cleared or warmed, so the precise cache setup should be documented for reproducibility.

### How to interpret the metrics together

Use **median** to describe typical runtime, **p95** to assess the slower end of normal performance against a latency target, and **worst** to show the slowest run observed in this small sample. None of these measures tells you whether the report is factually correct; report quality needs separate evaluation.

## 8. Limitations

1. Each condition has only 10 runs. p95 estimates are therefore unstable and should be treated as preliminary.
2. The benchmark measures latency and successful completion, not factual accuracy, citation correctness overall, or red-flag fairness.
3. LLM cost was not tracked.
4. Stage timings are reported as separate summaries and do not necessarily sum to the total median.
5. The logs do not fully describe the cache setup.

## 9. Recommendations

1. Run more trials per condition to make p95 estimates more reliable; consider reporting p50, p95 and p99 over repeated batches.
2. Record exactly which caches are cleared or warmed.
3. Measure LLM cost per report and total cost for each benchmark batch.
4. Evaluate report quality separately using a proper evaluation set, including factual field matching, citation quality and red-flag fairness.
5. Focus latency optimisation on the LLM report-generation stage, since it dominates total runtime.

## 10. Conclusion

The system meets the stated **p95 < 30 seconds** latency target in all four benchmark conditions. Template-based reports are substantially faster, while LLM-based reports have a median runtime of about 3.2 seconds. For LLM reports, narrative generation is the main bottleneck; warm caching makes research faster but has little effect on total latency.

These results support a conclusion about **performance**, not overall accuracy or report quality.
